# CHURN REDUCTION INITIATIVE
## Executive Summary

### Situation
Customer churn is our largest preventable revenue loss driver, costing $2.0M annually. Our current churn rate sits at 7%, significantly lagging the SaaS industry benchmark of 4%. Root-cause analysis reveals that customer support response delay is the primary driver of customer departures. Addressing support bottlenecks presents an immediate, high-ROI revenue recovery opportunity.

### Key Findings
- **Response Speed Dictates Retention:** Customers receiving support within 2 hours churn at just 3%. Customers waiting over 24 hours churn at 12%—a 4x increase in attrition.
- **Support Velocity Deficit:** Our support team currently averages a 6-hour response time, missing the critical 2-hour retention window.
- **High-Value Exposure:** Enterprise customers (>$10K annual spend) churn at 15% when experiencing delayed support, accelerating revenue loss.

### Business Risks
- **Revenue Attrition:** Doing nothing guarantees a $2.0M annual revenue leak, with high-margin enterprise accounts facing the greatest risk.
- **Competitive Defection:** Competitors with faster support SLAs are actively poaching our dissatisfied enterprise accounts.
- **Escalating Operational Load:** Ticket volume grew 40% year-over-year while resolution times degraded, signaling severe team burnout.

### Recommendations
1. **Hire 2 Support Engineers ($200K/year):** Reduces average response time from 6 hours to under 2 hours. Expected annual churn recovery: $400K (2x ROI in Year 1).
2. **Establish a 2-Hour SLA ($0):** Implement daily tracking and priority queue rules to enforce accountability starting Jan 1.
3. **Dedicated Enterprise Support Lane ($50K engineering):** Route $10K+/year customers to priority lanes by Feb 1, cutting enterprise churn by 50%.

### Decision Needed
Approve $200K in support headcount budget and $50K in engineering resources by **Dec 15**. Total investment: $250K. Projected Year 1 return: **$400K in retained revenue** (Net Benefit: $150K).
